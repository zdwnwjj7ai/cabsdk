How is Polycystic Ovary Syndrome diagnosed using ultrasound?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is diagnosed on ultrasound by evaluating key features of the ovaries, including follicle count, follicle size, and overall ovarian volume. According to the Rotterdam criteria, an ovary is considered polycystic if it contains 12 or more follicles measuring 2–9 mm in diameter, or if the ovarian volume exceeds 10 mL. Ultrasound is typically used alongside blood tests and a review of symptoms — such as irregular periods, acne, or excess hair growth — to confirm a PCOS diagnosis.

If you're experiencing symptoms that could point to PCOS, early insight matters. Hormonal imbalances left unaddressed can affect fertility, metabolism, and long-term health. Explanation

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) is a condition that affects the ovaries. Doctors use ultrasound to help diagnose it. An ultrasound is like a special camera that lets doctors see inside the body.

Key Features on Ultrasound

Follicles: In PCOS, the ovaries have many tiny sacs called follicles. On an ultrasound, doctors look for 12 or more of these follicles in each ovary.

Ovary Size: The ovaries are often larger than usual in PCOS, so doctors check if the ovary volume is greater than 10 cubic centimeters.

These ultrasound features help doctors decide if someone might have PCOS, but they also consider other signs and symptoms, like irregular periods or hormone levels, to make a full diagnosis.

# Why the TikTok Trend ‘Bone Smashing’ Is Dangerous, According to Doctors

IF THERE’S ONE THING medical experts want you to know about the latest TikTok trend of “bone smashing,” it’s that you definitely shouldn’t try it.

“It’s very dangerous,” says cosmetic surgeon Ariel Ostad, M.D. “This is not a good idea.”

Bone smashing refers to people hitting themselves in the face with hammers and other objects with the hope of changing their facial structure and improving their looks. TikTok videos tagged #bonesmashing have been viewed more than 353 million times. But, it's unclear whether people are actually smashing their faces.

“As a plastic surgeon, it terrifies me that people are attempting to smash their facial bones in order to enhance their appearance,” says Lyle Leipziger, M.D., chief of plastic surgery at North Shore University Hospital and Long Island Jewish Medical Center.

The idea behind bone smashing, according to TikTokkers, is that by potentially breaking or fracturing the bones in your face, you could trigger bone growth and give yourself a more chiseled look.

Breaking your own bones is obviously not a good idea—not only would it cause intense pain, but it could also possible disfigurement and other serious health problems, Dr. Ostad says.

Bone smashing is said to be linked to incel culture and a part of the “looksmaxing” trend. Looksmaxing refers to someone (usually young men) striving to improve their physical appearance in any way possible. It can include measures like cosmetic surgery, excessive exercise, and DIY efforts, like bone smashing.

Bone smashing is also linked to mewing, another social media trend where you train yourself to reposition your tongue’s resting place to change your facial features.

Here’s a look at why bone smashing is a dangerous trend that’s best avoided and how it can harm your appearance and your health.

Proponents of bone smashing often cite the 19th-century Wolff’s Law, which says that bones will adapt to stresses placed on them. But, Dr. Leipziger says they’re misunderstanding the idea.

“In general, bones may undergo change and remodeling in response to certain physical stressors,” he says. “Examples of good mechanical stress that can improve bone health are walking, general exercise, and weightlifting.”

Reasonable tension on the bones, like strength training, can cause a strain on your muscles and lead to bone strength, which is beneficial, Dr. Ostad adds.

Wolff’s Law focuses on bone remodeling, which is the idea that your bones change over your lifetime as you adapt to bone stress and develop new bone material. It’s sometimes applied to bone care treatments for osteoporosis or bone fractures and used in physical therapy.

Self-inflicted bone-breaking doesn’t fall under Wolff’s Law, however. Bone smashing is unlikely to strengthen your bones or change your appearance in a positive way, doctors say. Instead, it will likely just cause problems.

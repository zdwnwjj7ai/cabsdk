# 5 Brands for Guys Who Want to Look Good While Doing Good

Every fashion brand has a reason for doing what it does. And while we’re all for the ones that are dedicated to helping us look sharp, we’re especially impressed by companies that make an impact beyond style and put charitable giving at the heart of what they’re all about.

Here are five of our favorite do-good, look-good brands to shop.

How Kenneth Cole Helps Veterans Transition Back Into the Workforce

Kenneth Cole, the man, and Kenneth Cole, the brand, are big on giving back. Since the label launched in 1982, it’s tackled causes like AIDS, homelessness, and natural disasters.

In 2015, the brand added veterans to that list and set out to help former soldiers transition back to civilian life with the launch of Awearness Kenneth Cole, a line of suits, ties, dress shirts, and tailored pants.

For each item purchased, a contribution is made to Hire Heroes USA, a nonprofit that helps veterans find jobs, and Help USA, which provides transitional housing for those who need it. Editors’ Pick: The Awear-Tech by Awearness Kenneth Cole suit, pictured above, delivers the comfort of gym gear in suit form. The performance fabric offers stretch and temperature-control technology that takes moisture away when you’re heated and insulates when you’re cold.

How Good Man Brand Prepares the Leaders Of Tomorrow

Celebrities aren’t new to the giving-back game, but that doesn’t mean we’re not impressed when famous guys take on worthy causes. The latest example? Seattle Seahawks quarterback Russell Wilson co-founded Good Man Brand earlier this year. The line takes basics and elevates them by paying close attention to comfort, quality, and style.

If that alone doesn’t make Good Man Brand your go-to for button downs, henleys, and chinos, the brand’s tie-in with Wilson’s Why Not You Foundation will. For every item purchased, $3 is donated to the foundation, which inspires confidence and leadership among young people facing difficult situations such as domestic violence or cancer. Editors’ Pick: Good Man Brand’s take on the classic crewneck sweatshirt, pictured above, isn’t just for lounging around on the weekends. Thanks to its slim fit and French terry fabric, it’s thin enough to layer under a blazer. ($128, thegoodmanbrand.com)

How Sevenly Lets You Support Your Cause of Choice

Giving back has been a pillar of Sevenly since it launched in 2011. The brand hosts monthly, weekly, and daily campaigns to tackle some of the most pressing issues facing our world.

Here’s how it’s done: You shop the collection of graphic T-shirts and sweatshirts. Seven percent of your purchase—or $7 if a campaign is live—is donated to The Sevenly Foundation, which shovels the funds to nonprofit partners. The best part is the site makes it easy to shop by cause. Passionate about raising autism awareness? Snag a “Dare to be Different” tee to benefit people living with the disorder or caring for someone who is.

Want to promote human rights and freedom around the globe? Shop the Kindness cause. In total, the company has raised over $4.4 million—and the number keeps growing, as you can see in the tally on the upper right-hand corner of the site. Editors’ Pick: Pair this T-shirt, pictured above, with a leather jacket and well-worn jeans. Then, add even more style by throwing on a scarf. (Here are three ways to tie it.) ($26, sevenly.org)

How Warby Parker Helps the World Work

The eyewear brand is pretty much a shoo-in on any list of charitable companies, and for good reason: Its buy-a-pair-give-a-pair program has distributed over 2 million pairs of glasses around the globe.

Not only does the brand make it more affordable for you to get your hands on new specs, it also brings 20/20 vision to some of the 703 million people who currently don’t have access to eyewear. Rather than solely donating glasses, which the brand says is a temporary solution, Warby Parker gives money to nonprofit partners that train people in impovershed areas to give eye exams and sell affordable eyewear. Taking this approach encourages job growth and makes a more lasting economic impact. Editors’ Pick: The foggy gray color on these frames goes well with literally anything. ($95, warbyparker.com)

How Keen Supports the Outdoors

[While Doing](https://lumina-dermis.sunsimiaosd.com/)

Keen is all about giving you the tools you need to get outside, so it makes sense that the brand would also make it a priority to protect those outdoor spaces where we play.

Its commitment to charitable giving can be traced back to the company’s early days when it donated its entire marketing budget of about $1 million to the relief efforts of the 2004 South Asian tsunami. Since then, Keen has donated more than $10 million to nonprofits through the initiative it calls Keen Effect.

The effort expanded in 2013 with a grant program that invited people—Keen employees and outsiders alike—to nominate deserving nonprofits with a conservation focus.

So far, the grantee arm of the program has donated more than $322,000 to organizations that are doing everything from protecting clean water to introducing kids to healthy homegrown food.

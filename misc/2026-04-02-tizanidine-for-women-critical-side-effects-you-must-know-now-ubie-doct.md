Tizanidine for Women: Side Effects, Safety & Your Next Steps

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Tizanidine side effects in women commonly include drowsiness, dizziness, dry mouth, fatigue, low blood pressure, and slow heart rate. Rare but serious risks include liver injury. Key drug interactions that increase side effects include hormonal birth control with ethinyl estradiol, ciprofloxacin, fluvoxamine, alcohol, and blood pressure or sedative medications. Tizanidine is generally avoided during pregnancy and used cautiously while breastfeeding.

Important next steps: review all medications with your clinician, ask about liver function tests, take doses when you can rest, avoid alcohol, and never stop tizanidine suddenly. Seek urgent care for signs of liver problems, fainting, or severe drowsiness, and plan carefully around contraception, conception, or breastfeeding.

Because tizanidine side effects can overlap with many other conditions—and symptoms like fatigue, dizziness, or nausea may signal something beyond medication effects—it's worth getting clarity fast. Explanation

If you've been prescribed tizanidine, you likely have questions about how it works, what side effects to expect, and whether it's safe for you as a woman. That's completely reasonable. Tizanidine is a commonly used medication, but women may experience certain effects differently depending on hormones, body composition, other medications, and overall health.

Here's what you need to know—clearly and honestly—so you can make informed decisions about your care.

What Is Tizanidine?

Tizanidine is a muscle relaxant. It is most often prescribed to treat:

Spasticity caused by multiple sclerosis (MS)

Spinal cord injuries

Stroke

Certain neurological conditions

Severe muscle spasms

It works by acting on the central nervous system. Specifically, it reduces signals in the brain and spinal cord that cause muscles to tighten or spasm.

Unlike some muscle relaxants, tizanidine works relatively quickly and has a short duration of action. This allows it to be taken only when needed in some cases, rather than around the clock.

Why Women May Experience Tizanidine Differently

Women may respond differently to medications for several reasons:

Differences in body fat and muscle mass

Hormonal fluctuations

Slower or faster drug metabolism

Low Blood Pressure

Tizanidine and Pregnancy

Tizanidine is generally not recommended during pregnancy unless clearly needed. There is limited human research, and animal studies suggest potential risk.

If you are:

Pregnant

Trying to conceive

Not using contraception

Talk with your doctor before starting or continuing tizanidine.

Do not stop the medication suddenly without guidance, as this can cause withdrawal symptoms.

Tizanidine and Breastfeeding

It is not fully known how much tizanidine passes into breast milk. Because it affects the central nervous system, doctors often recommend caution.

Discuss the risks and benefits with your healthcare provider if you are breastfeeding.

Hormonal Birth Control and Tizanidine

This is important.

Certain oral contraceptives (especially those containing ethinyl estradiol) can increase tizanidine levels in the body. That means:

Stronger side effects

More sedation

Greater risk of low blood pressure

If you use hormonal birth control, your doctor may:

Prescribe a lower tizanidine dose

Monitor you more closely

Consider alternative treatments

Always tell your provider about all medications and supplements you take.

Withdrawal and Stopping Tizanidine

Do not stop tizanidine abruptly, especially if you've been taking it regularly.

Sudden withdrawal may cause:

High blood pressure

Rapid heart rate

Muscle tightness

Anxiety

Tremors

If you need to stop, your doctor will taper the dose gradually.

Is Tizanidine Safe Long-Term?

For many women, yes—when properly monitored.

Long-term safety depends on:

Liver monitoring

Avoiding interacting medications

Using the lowest effective dose

Regular follow-ups with your doctor

Some women use it only during flare-ups. Others use it daily for chronic neurological conditions. Your care plan should be individualized.

Your Next Steps

If you are considering or currently taking tizanidine, here's what to do:

Review all medications and supplements with your doctor.

Ask whether liver monitoring is needed.

Final Thoughts

Tizanidine can be an effective treatment for muscle spasticity and severe spasms in women. While side effects like drowsiness and low blood pressure are common, serious complications are uncommon when the medication is used appropriately and monitored carefully.

The goal isn't to fear the medication—but to use it wisely.

With proper dosing, awareness of interactions, and regular communication with your healthcare provider, tizanidine can be a helpful part of your treatment plan.

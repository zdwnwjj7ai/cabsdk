Explanation

Telogen Effluvium (TE) is a common form of temporary hair shedding often triggered by physical or emotional stress. If you've noticed more hair on your pillow, in the shower drain, or on your brush, you're not alone—and there are proven ways to support regrowth. One promising option is Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy, also known as PRP for stress hair loss. Below, we'll explore how PRP works, what to expect, and practical tips to help you feel empowered on your journey back to healthy hair.

What Is Telogen Effluvium?

Telogen Effluvium occurs when a larger-than-normal number of hair follicles enter the resting (telogen) phase, leading to increased shedding 2–3 months after a triggering event. Normally, about 10% of scalp hairs rest before falling out, but in TE, this can jump to 30–70%.

Common triggers include:

High psychological stress (bereavement, work pressure)

Acute or chronic illness (fever, infection)

Hormonal shifts (postpartum, thyroid imbalance)

Nutritional deficiencies (iron, zinc, protein)

Medications (blood thinners, certain antidepressants)

Although alarming, TE is usually reversible once the trigger is addressed. However, spotting signs early and taking targeted action can speed recovery.

What Is PRP Therapy?

Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) is an autologous treatment—meaning it uses your own blood—to harness growth factors that support tissue repair and hair follicle stimulation. In PRP:

A small amount of your blood is drawn.

It's spun in a centrifuge to concentrate platelets and growth factors.

The platelet-rich fraction is injected into thinning areas of your scalp.

Because it's your own biology, PRP carries minimal risk of allergic reaction and is well tolerated.

How PRP Works for Stress Hair Loss

PRP stimulates hair regrowth by:

Releasing growth factors (PDGF, TGF-β, VEGF) that boost follicle health

Promoting angiogenesis, forming new blood vessels to nourish follicles

Reducing inflammation around hair roots

Prolonging the anagen (growth) phase of hair

These mechanisms can help "wake up" follicles stalled in telogen and encourage stronger, thicker regrowth.

The PRP Procedure: Step by Step

Consultation and Assessment• Review your medical history and TE triggers• Scalp exam and possibly trichoscopy (magnified imaging)

Blood Draw• Usually 10–20 ml from your arm

Centrifugation• Separates plasma into platelet-poor and platelet-rich layers

Scalp Preparation• Cleansing and topical anesthetic or vibration device for comfort

Injection• Multiple small injections into thinning areas, spaced 1 cm apart

Post-Procedure Care• Avoid washing for 24 hours• Mild scalp soreness or redness may occur

Most sessions take 30–45 minutes. A typical course is 3–4 monthly treatments, followed by maintenance every 3–6 months.

Benefits of PRP for Stress Hair Loss

Minimally invasive with low downtime

Autologous—no risk of donor-related reactions

Supports natural regrowth rather than just concealing loss

Can be combined with other therapies (topical minoxidil, low-level laser therapy)

Improves scalp health, reducing inflammation and creating a regenerative environment

Risks and Considerations

PRP is generally safe, but it's important to discuss potential downsides:

Mild pain, swelling, or bruising at injection sites

Temporary headache or scalp tightness

Rare infection if aseptic technique isn't maintained

Variable response—some individuals see less regrowth than others

Who may not be a good candidate:

Platelet disorders or low platelet counts

Active scalp infections

Certain blood-clotting disorders

Uncontrolled chronic diseases

Always disclose your full medical history to your provider.

What to Expect: Timeline and Results

Weeks 0–4: Scalp may feel tender; minimal visible change.

Months 2–3: Gradual reduction in shedding; early new hair "peach fuzz."

Months 4–6: Noticeable thickening, improved hair density.

6+ Months: Full benefit of initial series; maintenance PRP optional.

Results vary based on age, overall health, and TE severity. Consistency is key.

# The Truth About High-End Denim

[About High-End Denim](https://metabolic-horizon.bbsxyxy.com/blog/1917240119.html)

You could fly across the country. You could buy a new iPhone. You could spend a night at a badass hotel. Or you could buy one pair of jeans.

There’s a lot you could do with $300—and if you’re like most guys, buying seemingly overpriced denim isn’t at the top of your list. But as we explained in In Defense of $300 Jeans, luxury jeans aren’t just denim, they’re an investment in high quality products, produced in your own backyard.

Besides, if jeans are the backbone of your wardrobe, then aren’t they worth your hard-earned dollar? “Many people will spend thousands on a suit that they’ll wear a few times a year, but won’t spend $250 on a pair of jeans that they’ll wear everyday,” says Matt Eddmenson, codesigner at Imogene + Willie, a high-end denim brand based in Nashville.

Still, for such a basic, everyman item, jeans can be complicated and confusing. It’s a valid question: why spend a few hundred bucks when you can spend a fraction of that? That’s why we’ve broken down the top 5 strengths of expensive denim. The benefits may surprise you.

The fabric

[High-End Denim](https://peplawllc.com/blog/7935242123.html)

Consider selvedge the holy grail of denim. Made on old, narrow looms from the late 1800s, the thread is continuous, creating a natural, or “self-edge.” “It’s a self-closed edge that doesn’t unravel,” says Eddmenson. On the other hand, larger looms process multiple threads; when one is finished, another is waiting. This speeds up production, but produces loose ends that need an overlock stitch to prevent unraveling. In other words, true denim has been sacrificed for manufacturers' financial gain.

The fit

[The Truth About](https://barrierwatch.bbsxyxy.com/blog/4219284613.html)

Low-end denim companies typically make cookie-cutter fits that work for everyone. Theoretically. Problem is, like off-the-rack suits, they don’t always fit everyone well. Many high-end brands (like Imogene + Willie) include custom tailoring with every purchase.

The attention to detail

Most luxury denim brands are based in the United States, and yes, you’re going to pay more for it. Do a quick comparison of the cost of labor between the U.S. and China and you’ll see why—a living wage and safe working conditions cost more. But you’re also paying for profound attention to detail and craftsmanship—and pride in the product.

“Every yard of fabric undergoes a rigorous investigation,” says Matthew Saam director of men’s design at J Brand. “And if a pair isn’t perfect, it won’t see a store shelf.” Sure, lower-end denim companies have standards, too, but they’re managed by large factories overseas. “Our jeans are made in downtown L.A., and we treat them like our children,” says Saam.

The character

The best part of high-quality denim—besides that they’ll last so long you won’t need to replace ‘em for years—is that they last long enough to develop true character. A great pair of selvedge jeans will begin rigid, but gradually mold to your body, developing wear and tear unique to your lifestyle and shape. “Each rip, stain, and crease adds personality to the jean,” explains Eric Goldstein, co-owner and founder of The Jean Shop in New York City. “The one-of-a-kind wear over the course of a few years really says something about the life you live.”

What Is NMN?

NMN is a natural compound found in small amounts in foods like broccoli, edamame, and avocado. It's a direct precursor to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), a critical molecule in every cell.

NAD+ helps our cells produce energy (ATP) by powering mitochondria.

It's essential for DNA repair, stress responses, and communication between cellular systems.

NAD+ levels decline as we age, and restoring those levels is a key strategy in anti-aging research.

The Role of NAD+ in Longevity Genetics

NMN and longevity genetics are tightly linked because of NAD+'s influence on a group of proteins called sirtuins. Sirtuins are often called "longevity genes" for their ability to:

Promote DNA repair and genome stability

Regulate inflammation

Improve mitochondrial function

Control metabolic pathways, including glucose and fat metabolism

Research Highlights on NMN and Longevity

Animal Studies

Mice: Supplementation with NMN restored NAD+ levels, improved insulin sensitivity, and enhanced mitochondrial function. Older mice treated with NMN showed increased physical activity and better eye function.

Lifespan: Some rodent studies report modest increases in median lifespan and improved markers of healthspan (the period of life spent free from serious disease).

Early Human Trials

Safety: Small-scale studies report that daily doses of up to 500 mg NMN are well tolerated in healthy adults, with minimal side effects such as mild gastrointestinal discomfort.

Biomarkers: Participants saw rises in NAD+ blood levels and improvements in markers for cardiovascular and metabolic health.

While human data are still emerging, these results suggest NMN may be a promising tool in supporting your body's natural longevity pathways.

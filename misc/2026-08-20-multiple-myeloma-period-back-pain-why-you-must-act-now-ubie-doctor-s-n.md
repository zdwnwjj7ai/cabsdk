Back pain that intensifies during your period is most commonly linked to menstrual disorders such as dysmenorrhea, endometriosis, or uterine fibroids—not multiple myeloma. Hormonal changes and uterine contractions during menstruation frequently cause referred pain to the lower back. While persistent or severe back pain always warrants medical evaluation, cyclical pain tied to your period is rarely a sign of cancer.

Because back pain has many possible causes—ranging from muscular strain to gynecological conditions—identifying the right next step can feel overwhelming. Explanation

Menstrual pain and low back pain are often connected. During your period, you might feel more back pain because of menstrual disorders. These disorders can cause the muscles in your lower back to react, leading to more pain.

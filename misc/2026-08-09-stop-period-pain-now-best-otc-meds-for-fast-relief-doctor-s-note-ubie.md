Over-the-counter medications are among the most effective first-line treatments for period pain. Options include:

Ibuprofen (Advil, Motrin) — an NSAID that reduces inflammation

Naproxen (Aleve) — a longer-acting NSAID

Acetaminophen / Paracetamol (Tylenol) — blocks pain signals in the brain

NSAIDs work by lowering prostaglandins, the compounds that trigger uterine cramping, while acetaminophen targets pain perception directly. For best results, take them at the first sign of cramps.

However, not all period pain is normal. Severe, worsening, or unusual symptoms can signal underlying conditions like endometriosis, fibroids, or PCOS — issues that OTC pills won't fix. Understanding the root cause is the fastest path to lasting relief, so before assuming it's "just cramps," take a free, instant, online symptom check to see what may be causing your pain and what to do next.

Explanation

Period pain can be uncomfortable, but there are over-the-counter options that may help. Over-the-counter medications such as ibuprofen, naproxen, acetaminophen, or paracetamol can provide relief from period pain and are commonly used for this purpose. These medications work by reducing inflammation (including reducing the production of prostaglandins, which are chemicals in the body that cause pain and inflammation during menstruation) and/or blocking pain signals.

How Ibuprofen and Naproxen Work

Reduces Prostaglandins: Ibuprofen and naproxen help lower the levels of prostaglandins, leading to less pain and discomfort during periods.

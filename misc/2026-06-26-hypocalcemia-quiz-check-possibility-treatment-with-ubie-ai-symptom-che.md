What is Hypocalcemia?

Hypocalcemia occurs when blood calcium levels are too low. It's often caused by abnormal levels of parathyroid hormone (PTH) or vitamin D in the body.

Typical Symptoms of Hypocalcemia

Muscle weakness after hyperventilating or crying hard

Seizure

Abnormal facial sensation

Decreased motivation

Tingling or prickling sensation without touching the skin

Numbness in the fingers

Numbness or tingling

Numbness of the face or mouth

Diagnostic Questions for Hypocalcemia

Your doctor may ask these questions to check for this disease:

Do you have muscle weakness after hyperventilating or crying hard?

Have you experienced any seizures?

Are you experiencing any abnormal facial sensations?

Have you been experiencing decreased motivation recently?

Do you feel tingling or prickling sensations in your skin?

Treatment of Hypocalcemia

Oral calcium supplements are the most common treatment for hypocalcemia. Vitamin D supplements can help with calcium absorption. Treating the cause of hypocalcemia is also important.

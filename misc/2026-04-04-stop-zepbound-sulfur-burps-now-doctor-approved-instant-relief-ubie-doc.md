What Are Sulfur Burps?

Sulfur burps happen when bacteria in your gut produce hydrogen sulfide gas, which smells like rotten eggs. This gas is expelled through belching and sometimes even through flatulence. Occasional sulfur burps can be annoying but harmless. Frequent episodes may signal:

Imbalanced gut bacteria

Digestive enzyme deficiency

Food intolerances

Overgrowth of certain bacteria (small intestinal bacterial overgrowth, or SIBO)

Why Zepbound Might Trigger Them

Zepbound works by mimicking gut hormones to regulate appetite and blood sugar. This slowdown in stomach emptying can:

Give certain bacteria more time to ferment proteins and sulfur-containing foods

Alter your digestive enzyme activity

Change gut motility, leading to gas buildup

While most people tolerate Zepbound well, if your diet is rich in sulfur foods (eggs, broccoli, cauliflower, red meat), you may notice more foul burps.

Instant Relief: 5 Quick Fixes

If you need immediate relief from sulfur burps, try these at-home remedies. Many users report significant improvement within minutes to an hour.

Sip Warm Lemon Water

Mix juice of half a lemon in 8 oz warm water.

Lemon's acidity can neutralize odor-causing gases and kickstart digestion.

Take an Antacid or Bismuth Subsalicylate

Over-the-counter antacids (calcium carbonate) can neutralize excess stomach acid.

Bismuth subsalicylate (e.g., Pepto-Bismol) binds hydrogen sulfide and soothes the gut lining.

Chew Sugar-Free Peppermint or Ginger Gum

Stimulates saliva, which contains digestive enzymes.

Peppermint and ginger can relax the digestive tract and reduce gas.

Try Activated Charcoal

Charcoal tablets can adsorb gas and odors in the gut.

Take according to package directions (usually 1–2 capsules).

Use Digestive Enzymes

If your body struggles to break down proteins or sulfur-rich foods, an enzyme supplement may help.

Look for broad-spectrum digestive enzyme formulas containing protease, lipase, and alpha-galactosidase.

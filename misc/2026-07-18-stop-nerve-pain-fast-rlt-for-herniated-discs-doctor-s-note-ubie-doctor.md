Understanding Herniated Discs and Nerve Pain

A herniated (slipped) disc occurs when the soft, gel-like center of an intervertebral disc pushes through a tear in the tougher exterior. This can compress nearby nerves, resulting in:

Radiating pain (e.g., sciatica)

Numbness or tingling in arms or legs

What Is Red Light Therapy (RLT)?

Red light therapy, also called low-level laser therapy or photobiomodulation, uses specific wavelengths (600–1000 nm) of red and near-infrared light to penetrate tissues. Proposed mechanisms include:

Boosting cellular energy (ATP) in mitochondria

Reducing inflammation by modulating cytokine production

Promoting collagen synthesis for tissue repair

Increasing local blood flow

Clinical studies show RLT can shorten recovery time, lessen pain, and enhance function in musculoskeletal injuries.

Evidence for RLT in Disc and Nerve Conditions

While research on herniated discs is still evolving, several credible studies highlight benefits:

A randomized trial in patients with chronic low back pain found that red/near-infrared light therapy significantly reduced pain intensity and improved functional scores after 4 weeks of treatment.

Systematic reviews of photobiomodulation report moderate to strong evidence for pain relief in various musculoskeletal disorders, including nerve entrapment syndromes.

Animal models demonstrate accelerated tissue repair and reduced inflammatory markers in intervertebral discs exposed to near-infrared light.

Integrating Core Work for Spinal Support

Strong, stable core muscles help distribute spinal loads evenly, reducing stress on injured discs. Core work focuses on the deep stabilizers and functional muscle chains:

Transverse abdominis

Multifidus

Pelvic floor

Diaphragm coordination

Key exercises include:

Pelvic tilts: Lie on your back with knees bent. Gently press your lower back into the floor by engaging your abdominals. Hold 5 seconds, repeat 10–15 times.

Bird dogs: On hands and knees, extend one arm forward and opposite leg back. Maintain a neutral spine, hold 3–5 seconds, switch sides. 8–12 reps.

Modified planks: From elbows and knees (or toes if tolerated), keep spine straight. Hold 10–30 seconds, gradually increasing as strength improves.

Glute bridges: Lie on back, feet flat, lift hips by squeezing glutes and engaging core. Hold 2–3 seconds, lower slowly. 10–15 reps.

Consistent, pain-free core training enhances postural control, supports disc healing, and reduces risk of re-injury.

Safety Considerations and Contraindications

Red light therapy and core exercises are generally safe when done correctly, but be mindful of:

Unstable spinal fractures or severe spinal instability

Active infections or open wounds near the treatment area

Cancerous lesions in the region

Pregnancy (consult an obstetrician before RLT)

Photosensitivity from medications (e.g., certain antibiotics)

Always follow device manufacturer guidelines and start core work under the guidance of a physical therapist if unsure.

When to Seek Professional Help

While conservative measures help most people, certain red flags require prompt medical evaluation:

Sudden loss of bowel or bladder control

Severe, unrelenting pain at rest

Rapidly progressing leg weakness or numbness

Fever accompanying back pain

If you encounter any of these, please speak to a doctor or visit the nearest emergency department.

For non-urgent concerns, you can also speak to your primary care provider or spine specialist about advanced imaging or referral for interventional treatments.

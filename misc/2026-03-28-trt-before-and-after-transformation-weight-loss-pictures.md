Before TRT: What Does Low T Look Like?

Most men don’t know their testosterone is low until they think about all the symptoms that have been quietly stacking up for years.

Testing your testosterone levels can confirm what’s been going on: the energy that’s harder to find, the muscle that won’t come back, the libido that’s faded. If total testosterone falls below 300 ng/dL, or if symptoms are present at higher levels, that’s low testosterone (Low T), aka hypogonadism.1

Persistent fatigue or low energy2

After TRT: Benefits & Timeline

Men starting TRT don’t wake up transformed overnight — but the timeline tends to move faster than most expect. Many notice benefits within the first 3 to 6 weeks, with more significant physical changes building over months of sustained treatment. Here’s around how long it takes for testosterone to start working:

1–2 weeks: Mental clarity,11 more energy.12 The earliest changes are often subtle but distinct. Some men notice a kind of fog starting to clear. These early shifts are often the first signal the therapy is working.

3–6 weeks: Stronger sex drive13, improved mood14. Libido is often one of the most noticeable early responses to TRT, alongside reduced fatigue and more stable mood. Sleep quality may also begin to improve around this window,15 with a positive downstream effect on daytime energy and motivation.

3–6 months: Muscle gain,16 weight loss.17 As testosterone levels stabilize, body composition changes become more apparent — lean muscle starts to build and fat (particularly visceral fat) begins to decrease.18

6–12 months: Sustained hormone improvements. Testosterone reaches a stable therapeutic range, and the cumulative benefits — in physical performance, metabolic health, and overall well-being — become more pronounced. Bone density may also begin to improve with longer-term treatment.

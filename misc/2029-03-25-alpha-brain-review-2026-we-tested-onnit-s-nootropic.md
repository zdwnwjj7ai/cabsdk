# Alpha BRAIN Review [2026] | We tested Onnit’s nootropic

Alpha BRAIN Review

Find out if Onnit’s nootropic safely boosts your brain function or if it might just give you some strange dreams.

Lee is a senior editor and writer at Innerbody Research. They received a bachelor's degree in Psychology with a focus in Neuroscience from Ohio Wesleyan University, and an MFA in Creative Writing from Northern Arizona University.

In this Review

We’ve all been there: tired, sluggish, drinking extra cups of coffee, and still feeling like you can’t get past a wall of fog. Difficulty with concentration, focus, and other cognitive tasks is growing more common,1 no matter the cause.2 3 In response to struggles like these, nootropics — substances designed to improve cognitive function — have been steadily increasing in popularity.4

Alpha BRAIN is a nootropic supplement created by Onnit, a health and wellness company based out of Austin, Texas. The supplement gained popularity when UFC commentator and ex-Fear Factor host Joe Rogan — who also owns shares in Onnit — advertised it on his podcast. Alpha BRAIN was designed to help sharpen your mind, improve memory, hone your word recall, and improve focus without using caffeine or other stimulants.

Sounds like a no-brainer to use, right? The answer to that might be more complex than it seems since nootropic supplements are still controversial in the scientific community. We looked into Alpha BRAIN to help you decide if it could be as good for you as it sounds.

Our Findings

Alpha BRAIN may have the potential to improve verbal memory; it has a clinically relevant ingredient list and two (self-funded) scientific studies, one of which was generally positive. However, proprietary blends obscure most ingredient dosages and make it difficult to truly estimate the chance of side effects from certain riskier nootropic ingredients.

How we evaluated Alpha BRAIN

To help you determine whether or not Alpha BRAIN is right for you, we focused on four primary criteria: safety, efficacy (how well it works), cost, and convenience.

Ultimately, we think that Alpha BRAIN is reasonably priced for a nootropic, but it has some issues with its formulation and how it presents itself to consumers, which gives us pause. Specifically, many of its ingredients are listed as part of proprietary blends that obscure how much (or, really, how little) is in each serving, and some potential side effects aren’t mentioned by Onnit. And even though Onnit has sponsored clinical studies on Alpha BRAIN, self-funded research can be affected by bias; we’d prefer to see independent third-party studies.

Anecdotally, some consumers report experiencing better verbal memory while taking Alpha BRAIN (consistent with some of the research on its ingredients). It’s relatively convenient to take, but it peaks quickly and wears off fast, and you shouldn’t take it more than once in 24 hours to minimize the risk of side effects. Don’t expect this to be life-changing, but if you need a little boost (particularly in verbal memory), Alpha BRAIN might be able to help.

Safety

Alpha BRAIN contains several acetylcholine-promoting ingredients. Acetylcholine is a neurotransmitter that influences your memory, attention, and focus, so it makes sense that it’s part of this nootropic. However, acetylcholine can be dangerous when there’s too much of it or not enough of the enzyme that turns it off.5 There’s no indication on the website that this can be dangerous. And while it’s unlikely that any serious side effects would occur given what we know about its ingredients, the fact that it’s an unaddressed possibility is concerning. Alpha BRAIN has been the subject of two clinical studies, neither of which looked at the safety of long-term regular use.

Similar to some nootropics competitors, like Focus Factor, many of Alpha BRAIN’s ingredients are listed as part of proprietary blends. That’s a sneaky move supplement manufacturers use when they need to list all ingredients but don’t want to tell you exactly how much of each goes into a product; listing ingredients as part of a blend lets them list how much is there in total but not individually. This makes it more challenging to determine both efficacy and safety.

For example, the Onnit Flow Blend contains 650mg of L-tyrosine, L-theanine, oat straw extract, and phosphatidylserine. Oat straw extract has no known health benefits, but the other three ingredients do. We don’t know how much of the blend is oat straw extract, so it could — dramatically and unlikely — be as much as 647mg of oat straw extract and 1mg of other ingredients. Not every ingredient in Alpha BRAIN is part of a blend, so we know a little about the formula, but there’s still so much obscured that it’s challenging to determine what’s going on.

Onnit tests ingredients and the final blend in-house for quality, purity, potency, and contamination but doesn’t share any results publicly. In comparison, competitor Mind Lab Pro does in-house and third-party testing and then posts batch-specific results on its website for public viewing.

Additionally, it’s unclear where Onnit’s phosphatidylserine is sourced from, but since it’s often derived from soy, Alpha BRAIN may be unsafe for those with soy allergies.29

Efficacy

It’s difficult to judge Alpha BRAIN’s efficacy because Onnit obscures a majority of its ingredients in a proprietary blend; we don’t know how much of each ingredient is in the formula. This obfuscation means that we can’t determine if things that are clinically more likely to work (like L-tyrosine) are present in high enough doses to make a difference. That being said, Alpha BRAIN does contain several ingredients with a decent amount of research behind them, implying that there may be some merit to Onnit’s grand claims.

Alpha BRAIN is one of the few nootropics on the market to have studies investigating its efficacy; Focus Factor Original, Mind Lab Pro, and Qualia Mind are some of the other nootropic stacks subjected to similar research. Alpha BRAIN’s 2015 research study showed it improved verbal memory recall in young adults, but a separate study on “mood, stress, and marksmanship” in soldiers showed it made no difference in cognitive performance.6 7 Neither study looked at long-term use or its effects on older adults or people struggling with cognition.

In customer reviews, users are mixed as to whether or not they experienced any differences in their thinking; about one in three users report no effect.

What is Alpha BRAIN?

Alpha BRAIN is the flagship product from Onnit, a Texas-based health and wellness company launched in 2011. It’s a nootropic designed to support your everyday cognitive function, including memory, mental processing speed, and focus. Alpha BRAIN was inspired by celebrity Joe Rogan, who was interested in the potential of nootropics to enhance cognitive function. Rogan, who says he’s taken the supplement regularly since before the product’s launch in 2010, claims Alpha BRAIN improves his word recall, focus, and performance to such an extent that he “doesn’t want to do anything without it.” (It’s worth mentioning that Rogan has shares in Onnit, meaning that the potential for bias in that statement is pretty high.)

Alpha BRAIN is BSCG-certified, meaning it doesn’t contain any ingredients banned by international athletic associations, nor does it have contact with any during the manufacturing process. It’s made in a facility compliant with current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP) regulations set by the FDA. These standards require that a company tests individual ingredients and product batches for:

Purity

Strength

Composition

Contamination

However, Onnit conducts all testing in-house at this facility and doesn’t share certificates of analysis (CoAs), which delineate testing results. In the future, we hope Onnit will consider being as transparent as competitor Mind Lab Pro and post CoAs for public viewing.

As a supplement, Alpha BRAIN isn’t held to the same FDA requirements as food or prescription medication.

How does Alpha BRAIN work?

Alpha BRAIN is a nootropic supplement that’s made of three proprietary blends:

Onnit Flow Blend (650mg): L-tyrosine, L-theanine, oat straw extract, phosphatidylserine

Onnit Focus Blend (240mg): Alpha-GPC, bacopa extract (100mg), toothed clubmoss extract (standardized to 1% huperzine A; 400mcg)

Onnit Fuel Blend (60mg): L-leucine, pterostilbene

It also contains 10mg of vitamin B6 and 350mg of cat’s claw (Uncaria tomentosa) extract. You won’t find any caffeine in Alpha BRAIN’s formula, making it a good choice for those particularly sensitive to stimulants. Thesis and Mind Lab Pro can also be good options for people looking to avoid caffeine; the former gives you the option of getting or skipping the caffeine, while the latter is always caffeine-free.

On the Alpha BRAIN product page, Onnit specifically walks through each of the individual ingredients, what it’s thought to do, and how the company sees it helping Alpha BRAIN accomplish its intended goal for your cognition. Each blend targets a different mechanism within your brain to help it function at its peak. Onnit Flow Blend is designed for “promoting alpha brain wave production,” Focus Blend for acetylcholine levels, and Fuel Blend for nervous system communication.

Alpha waves and flow states

Inside these capsules are three proprietary blends of vitamins, herbs, and amino acids designed to improve your cognitive functioning and increase the amount of time you spend in “flow states.” When you feel “in the groove” on a project, run, or piece of art and look up to see hours have flown by without you realizing it, you’ve been in a flow state. These states are often considered the pinnacle of productivity without compromising mental health. Neuroscientists think that flow states might be an internalization of cognitive control — movement from consciously thinking about your intent to do something to unconsciously making those choices for the same reasons.8

These flow states occur when the brain is functioning with alpha waves. This state of neurological activity can be seen on an EEG scan and is mostly found in the occipital lobe — the visual processing center in the brain — during quiet rest and deep relaxation. Alpha waves tend to disappear when you exert concentration on a task (replaced instead by beta waves, a higher frequency). Typically, alpha waves are associated with a lack of thinking and consolidation in what is and isn’t “on” in the brain.9

We know alpha waves might be associated with visual processing and working memory.10 Some studies suggest that problems with alpha waves might be related to things like social withdrawal and depression.11 They’ve been linked to creativity and improved focus, but not consistently.12 13 Like most things in the brain, we don’t conclusively know much about alpha waves or flow states compared to other bodily processes.

One study from 2022 suggested that not having enough acetylcholine might decrease the frequency and quality of alpha waves, so it’s not a huge jump that Alpha BRAIN’s highly acetylcholinergic formula could improve your “flow states.”14 However, that likely isn’t the biggest benefit you’ll experience.

Dreams

One of the more interesting effects of Alpha BRAIN — which doesn’t always come up in the advertising — is that it sometimes creates intensely vivid, often lucid, dreams. This is because the product primarily influences acetylcholine.

Acetylcholine is an important neurotransmitter that works with the parasympathetic nervous system to calm the body after a fight-or-flight response. It also orchestrates some release of other neurotransmitters (like serotonin and dopamine) that influence mood, hunger, memory, and more. Interestingly, acetylcholine seems to affect the start of REM sleep at night and can influence your dreams.15 16 Some researchers think it might be involved in muscle movement during sleep paralysis and dreams (though others think that might be from other neurotransmitters at work).17 Higher levels of acetylcholine during sleep also mean that you’ll experience REM for longer, which creates longer dreams that you’ll have a higher likelihood of remembering.

Alpha BRAIN contains several products that boost acetylcholine at the start and prevent it from leaving your system quickly, increasing your overall levels of acetylcholine from both ends of the process. Even those who don’t get a lot of everyday effects from Alpha BRAIN often find that their dreams are weirder because the product relies on acetylcholine to impact the body. It’s worth noting that other nootropics and prescription medications to improve cognitive function also cause observable changes in dreaming, so this side effect isn’t specific to Alpha BRAIN. Later on, we’ll explore acetylcholine and what ingredients in Alpha BRAIN influence it.

Alpha BRAIN dosing, side effects, and safety

Looking deeper into Alpha BRAIN’s ingredients reveals a clear problem with the supplement: not all of the ingredients have studies backing up Onnit’s claims. And those that do have positive research need to be present in large enough amounts to be effective. Since it isn’t clear how much of each ingredient is in Alpha BRAIN — and reaching out to the company doesn’t clear up that information — it’s difficult to say whether or not you can find most of the ingredients in levels high enough to make a difference.

The amount of vitamin B6, cat’s claw extract, and huperzine A (from the toothed clubmoss extract) are all appropriate based on clinical research, but they’re also three of only four ingredients with listed quantities per serving. (Bacopa extract appears to be short by about 200mg per serving.) Customers report mixed effects of Alpha BRAIN: some experience grand effects, and others experience none at all.

Onnit recommends taking two capsules daily with a light meal. It’s not uncommon to experience nausea, vomiting, and other GI problems when you first start taking Alpha BRAIN or any other nootropic, so be sure to take it with a meal to make it a bit easier on the stomach. However, if you experience other effects (such as muscle twitching or weakness, a sudden increase in tear or saliva production, or blurry vision), stop taking Alpha BRAIN and contact your doctor immediately. There is also a very low risk of kidney or liver damage, but if you have a history of kidney or liver disease, reach out to your doctor before trying Alpha BRAIN.

Insomnia may also be a sign that Alpha BRAIN isn’t right for you, but this side effect could also be caused by taking it too late in the afternoon. If you take Alpha BRAIN in the morning and still can’t sleep at night, you may find better luck with other nootropics. Thesis, for example, offers a nootropic blend called “Confidence” that includes ingredients to promote calm, like ashwagandha.18

Alpha BRAIN is not appropriate for pregnant or lactating people and anyone under the age of 18. And it may not be safe for those with soy allergies due to the unknown origins of the supplement’s phosphatidylserine (often made from soy).

Onnit Flow Blend

The first — and largest — blend found in Onnit’s Alpha BRAIN is the “Onnit Flow Blend.” This 650mg blend includes L-tyrosine, L-theanine, oat straw extract, and phosphatidylserine. Let’s go over some of the research behind these ingredients.

L-tyrosine

Tyrosine (present in Alpha BRAIN in its bioavailable form, L-tyrosine) is a non-essential amino acid with a big role. It’s one of the precursors necessary for producing the critical neurotransmitters epinephrine, norepinephrine, and dopamine. Not having enough tyrosine won’t ruin a workout, but it might stunt your cognition. Some studies have found that taking a tyrosine supplement helps to promote cognitive flexibility and improve short-term memory during stressful or cognitively demanding situations (which can deplete your natural norepinephrine and dopamine stores).19 20

Generally, researchers believe that tyrosine might not improve your everyday life, but it gives your brain the materials to keep you from floundering under pressure. Some people’s genetics may lead them to benefit more from supplemental tyrosine than others.21 More research is necessary to determine how it can help you long-term.22

Studies usually give participants about 2g of L-tyrosine. Unfortunately, 2g is more than double the amount of the entire Onnit Flow Blend, meaning there’s certainly not the same amount of L-tyrosine in Alpha BRAIN as found in successful studies. In comparison, Thesis’ Energy blend also doesn’t have anywhere near 2g, but it does include a definite 300mg, which is more than most other competitors on the market. (Mind Lab Pro has 175mg, while Qualia Mind and Noocube both have 250mg.)

L-theanine

If you’re familiar with the health benefits of green tea, you’re likely already familiar with L-theanine. This amino acid (also found in black tea and some mushrooms) promotes relaxation by lowering stress and anxiety while seemingly increasing cognitive capacity.23

L-Theanine appears to improve working memory and attention in people already at a deficit (particularly older adults and people with ADHD).24 25 Most studies look at L-theanine and caffeine together.26 However, Alpha BRAIN is stimulant-free and has no caffeine, so these possible benefits may not appear in the same ways. If you’re not sensitive to caffeine, then Thesis’ blends may be able to provide the synergetic potential of both caffeine and L-theanine if you opt for the caffeinated option. (Going “caffeine free” with Thesis removes both the caffeine and L-theanine content from the blends, unfortunately.)

Generally, participants in studies are given about 2.5mg/kg of L-theanine, meaning a person who weighs 150lb would take 170mg of L-theanine. In contrast to L-tyrosine, there could very well be around 170mg of L-theanine in the 650mg Onnit Flow Blend.

Oat straw extract

Oat straw (made from the stems and leaves of oats) is used in some nootropics for its supposed anti-anxiety features and theoretical ability to improve healthy cognitive functioning by increasing blood flow to the brain. However, no clinical research suggests it does any of these things.

Phosphatidylserine

Phosphatidylserine is a fat necessary for healthy cell membranes throughout your entire body, but it plays a vital role in the brain. It creates the outer coating of a nerve, called the myelin sheath, that helps it send information quickly (demyelinated neurons are the main cause of symptoms in multiple sclerosis and Guillain-Barre syndrome).

A large body of research suggests that phosphatidylserine supplements move effectively into the brain and can improve many cognitive properties, especially in older adults experiencing cognitive decline. On average, people find success taking anywhere from 100-800mg of phosphatidylserine daily, with a sweet spot of around 300mg.27 28

Insider Tip: Due to the inclusion of this ingredient in Alpha BRAIN, it may not be safe for those with soy allergies. It’s unclear what the source of Onnit’s phosphatidylserine is, but it’s very common for commercially produced phosphatidylserine to be derived from soy.29

Onnit Focus Blend

There are three main ingredients in the Onnit Focus Blend: alpha-GPC, bacopa extract, and toothed clubmoss extract.

Alpha-GPC

As one of the heavy hitters in nootropics, alpha-GPC is a precursor to acetylcholine, effectively increasing the amount circulating in the brain and nervous system. It’s related to choline and can be used interchangeably with the substance. Currently, medical researchers are studying its potential to reduce symptoms of Alzheimer’s disease and other forms of dementia. One study even suggested that long-term alpha-GPC consumption in mice kept aging-related cognitive decline to a minimum.52 However, more in-depth studies are needed on human subjects before this potential benefit can be considered legitimate or not.

Bacopa monnieri (100mg)

Bacopa monnieri is an aquatic plant related to ashwagandha that’s been used for centuries in traditional Ayurvedic medicine. It’s used for its purported memory-enhancing properties, and recent studies have started investigating whether or not it could be a viable ingredient in managing neurological disorders.30 And bacopa appears to influence dopamine cell receptors in rats, but we don’t know if that carries over to humans.31

It might also improve visual processing speeds and how well the brain learns and consolidates memory for up to 12 weeks.32 However, this study used 300mg of intravenous bacopa extract, which is absorbed much faster (and more thoroughly) than orally administered bacopa. Alpha BRAIN has about 100mg of Bacopa monnieri extract, so you may not find these results apply.

Toothed clubmoss

Toothed clubmoss (Huperzia serrata) extract contains one of the most interesting compounds in Alpha BRAIN: huperzine A. Among other things, huperzine A blocks the enzyme acetylcholinesterase. Unlike other neurotransmitters, which are reabsorbed into a neuron after a signal has finished sending, acetylcholine has its own enzyme (acetylcholinesterase) that sweeps out excess. In that regard, huperzine A acts almost like an SSRI (the most common antidepressant medication) but for acetylcholine. It’s another ingredient currently being studied to determine if it could be a suitable medication for mild Alzheimer’s disease.33 Acetylcholinesterase inhibitors are found in both Alzheimer’s medications and common pesticides.34

Too much acetylcholine can become dangerous very quickly. Alpha BRAIN’s toothed clubmoss extract is standardized to 1% huperzine A or 400mcg. This is the same dose commonly used in clinical trials, so it may be (theoretically) safe for daily use. However, it’s critical that you speak with your doctor first and don’t take more than one serving of Alpha BRAIN in a 24-hour window to limit your risk of acetylcholine overload.

Onnit Fuel Blend

The Onnit Fuel Blend, coming in at only 60mg, is the smallest blend by weight and on the ingredient list: it contains only L-leucine and pterostilbene.

L-leucine

Leucine (available in Alpha BRAIN as L-leucine) is an essential amino acid necessary for protein synthesis. It’s one of three essential branched-chain amino acids (BCAAs) that give skeletal muscles energy during exercise. Alpha BRAIN claims that L-leucine is included to improve nervous system communication, and research shows it can enhance axonal growth and regeneration.35

A 2021 study found that it also improved recovery in mice who suffered from a traumatic brain injury. However, the daily dose for the mice in this study was 100mg/kg. This would be equivalent to about 1,216mg per day for a 150lb human — over 20 times the size of the entire Onnit Fuel Blend.36

Pterostilbene

Pterostilbene is similar to resveratrol, a polyphenol you might’ve heard of commonly found in red wine. Scientists think that resveratrol (and pterostilbene by association) works in the body like an antioxidant, protecting against oxidative damage.

Oxidative stress is one of the biggest problems that affects the brain and central nervous system, considering the brain uses a tremendous amount of oxygen.37 However, not every brain is equally susceptible to oxidative stress; it’s mainly involved in neurodegenerative disorders like Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s, and Huntington’s disease.38 For those who struggle with neurodegenerative disease, pterostilbene appears to have positive outcomes thanks to its antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties.39

Additional ingredients in Alpha BRAIN

Other than the three blends, Alpha BRAIN contains two more active ingredients and a handful of inactive ones that make up its capsule and provide stabilization.

Vitamin B6 (10mg)

All B vitamins are important for nerve and brain health, but vitamin B6, also known as pyridoxine, is one of the most critical in this arena. This essential vitamin helps your brain:53 54

Create neurotransmitters, amino acids, and proteins

Regulate energy use in the brain

Maintain proper brain and immune system development and functioning

B6 deficiencies have been linked to cognitive impairment,40 seizures,41 migraine,42 anxiety,43 and depression (particularly in those taking oral contraceptives).44 45 Some experts theorize that vitamin B6 is so good for the brain because it’s one of the vitamins that helps the body break down homocysteine, an amino acid byproduct associated with dementia, heart disease, and stroke in high concentrations.55

You don’t make vitamin B6 naturally, so healthy adults need to get about 1.3mg of it daily.46 It isn’t clear if getting more than the recommended amount improves cognition beyond mitigating the effects of a deficiency.

If you’re concerned about a vitamin deficiency, we recommend speaking with your doctor to have tests done before supplementing.

Cat’s claw extract (350mg)

Uncaria tomentosa, also known as cat’s claw, is a woody vine found in and around the Amazon rainforest. South and Central American cultures have used it for centuries to treat ailments like:

Stomach problems

Arthritis

Inflammation

Fevers

While popular conceptions of the herb suggest that it might have anti-inflammatory and antioxidant properties, the research is still mixed.47

Still, cat’s claw is a common herbal ingredient in supplements for arthritis symptoms, and one study found it improved cognition, learning, and memory in middle-aged rats.48 The most effective dose for rats in the study was 30mg/kg, which would be about 365mg for a 150lb human, which is actually very close to the 350mg included in Alpha BRAIN. However, the research was done in rats, so more human studies are necessary to confirm this potential benefit.

Another study found 300mg (100mg three times a day) of cat’s claw improved the energy levels of terminal cancer patients without affecting their inflammation.49 Ultimately, there’s still a lot of research to be done to understand how cat’s claw extract works (or doesn’t).

Inactive ingredients

Each serving also contains an unknown amount of maltodextrin and rice hull concentrate, listed in the “other ingredients” section. Rice hull concentrate is a relatively effective prebiotic. While maltodextrin is 95% carbohydrate with a high glycemic index generally used for preserving the shelf life of a supplement, it’s also found in sports drinks to give your body a little extra carbohydrate boost. It may serve this purpose in Alpha BRAIN, too.

Studies on Alpha BRAIN

While we don’t know for sure if Alpha BRAIN works as advertised for everyone, researchers in a 2016 study found that after six weeks, people aged 18 to 35 who took Alpha BRAIN performed modestly (but significantly) better on neuropsychological tests measuring verbal memory and executive functioning than their peers who didn’t. They could recall words from their short-term working memory faster and longer than before, which is consistent with how the Onnit team discusses Alpha BRAIN.

The study attributes this success primarily to huperzine A but notes that most participants were already high-functioning healthy young adults (the average participant age was 23), so we don’t know how this might apply to people who are genuinely struggling with their cognition. It was published twice — in 2015 in the Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition and again in Human Psychopharmacology in 2016 with significant improvements. (Perhaps they’d started taking Alpha BRAIN between drafts.)

Another study from 2018 showed that Alpha BRAIN had no noticeable or statistically significant effects on soldiers’ mood, cognitive function, or marksmanship when taking the nootropic at a dose slightly higher than recommended (three capsules per day rather than two). This study was funded by Onnit, though the company didn’t work with the research team directly.

Because Onnit at least partially funded the research, the studies are at higher risk of bias. In the future, we hope to maybe see an independent study on Alpha BRAIN.

Alternatives to Alpha BRAIN

There are dozens of different nootropics out there, and all of them promise fantastic results. However, because we don’t know how a majority of these ingredients work for cognitive enhancement by themselves — let alone how they work together — it’s much harder to determine whether or not a supplement will truly help.

Some nootropics are focused, meaning they only include ingredients that might help improve one aspect of cognition (such as memory, focus, or creativity). Others, called generalized nootropics, are less focused. However, generalized nootropics tend to be less effective because they’re trying to do everything at once.

Frequently asked questions about Alpha BRAIN

Is Alpha BRAIN a scam or a gimmick?

Alpha BRAIN is a legitimate nootropic supplement. Is it perfect? No, but no nootropics are perfect. We understand so little about the human brain in general that no nootropic supplement will be perfect in this decade (if ever). Alpha BRAIN is short on some key ingredients, but a few that it does include should increase the amount of acetylcholine in your brain, which may improve some cognitive functions.

Alpha BRAIN seems to work best on verbal memory, so if you’re struggling to recall words or phrases, you may find success with Alpha BRAIN. It won’t make you a spelling bee winner or immediately grant you perfect marks in all of your classes, but it might help you hold onto your thoughts and focus a little more.

How long can you safely take Alpha BRAIN?

There haven’t been many studies looking at how long-term nootropic use affects the brain. The research studies that were performed using Alpha BRAIN had participants take the supplement daily for six weeks without any side effects, so it may be safe for about six weeks. However, because too much acetylcholine in your brain can be dangerous over the long term, we recommend pausing every few weeks to ensure you’re still feeling your best. Ultimately, you should speak with your doctor before taking Alpha BRAIN, and they can help you figure out a safe span of time.

If you ever start to feel cramps, increased saliva production, tear flow (that isn’t crying), muscle weakness, diarrhea, blurry vision, or random muscle twitching, stop taking Alpha BRAIN immediately and contact your doctor. Those may be signs of excessive acetylcholine accumulation.

When’s the best time to take Alpha BRAIN?

Onnit recommends taking Alpha BRAIN with a light meal. Taking it with breakfast ensures that you’ll feel the effects early enough in the day to not interfere with your sleep at night, and since it takes an average of 1-2 hours for it to begin working, you’ll feel it while you’re at work, in class, or going about your day. We don’t recommend downing Alpha BRAIN with a cup of coffee until you understand how Alpha BRAIN works with your body, as caffeine is also a potent nootropic.

How long does it take for Alpha BRAIN to take effect?

It takes anywhere from 30 minutes to three hours to feel Alpha BRAIN’s effects, but most people feel it between 1-2 hours after taking it. There’s some discussion about whether or not it needs time to build up in your brain to feel the full effects, but given its theoretical mechanism of action, that’s unlikely. You may run into some side effects if you take it every day for weeks on end.

How many times a day can I take Alpha BRAIN?

Take two capsules of Alpha BRAIN only once a day. Onnit explicitly states that you shouldn’t take more than one serving every 24 hours.

Does Alpha BRAIN make you jittery?

Alpha BRAIN has no stimulants — including the number-one culprit of nootropic-induced jitters, caffeine — so it shouldn’t make you feel jittery or jumpy. If it does, cut back to one capsule daily to see if a decrease in dose improves your experience.

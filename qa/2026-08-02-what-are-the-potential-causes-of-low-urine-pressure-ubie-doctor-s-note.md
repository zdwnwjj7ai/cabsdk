What are the potential causes of low urine pressure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Low urine pressure can be caused by aging, nerve problems, or bladder issues. These conditions can affect how well the bladder and urinary system work.

Low urine pressure can happen for a few reasons. In older people, it might be due to changes in the bladder and urinary system as they age. As people get older, their bladder muscles can weaken, and this can make it hard to push urine out with strong pressure.

Another cause is nerve problems, which can affect how the bladder works. If the nerves that control the bladder do not work right, it can make it hard to urinate normally. This can happen in some diseases that affect the nerves.

Lastly, there can be problems with the bladder itself. Sometimes, the bladder might not be able to contract well, which means it cannot push the urine out with enough force. This can lead to low urine pressure and slower urine flow.

Understanding these causes can help in finding the right treatment or management plan for low urine pressure.

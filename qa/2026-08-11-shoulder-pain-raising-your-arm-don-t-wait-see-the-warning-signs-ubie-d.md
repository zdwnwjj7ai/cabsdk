Shoulder pain when raising your arm is often caused by shoulder impingement or a rotator cuff tear—two of the most common shoulder conditions. Shoulder impingement occurs when tendons become pinched between the bones of the shoulder during arm movement, leading to inflammation and pain. A rotator cuff tear involves damage to the muscles or tendons that stabilize the shoulder joint, often resulting from overuse, injury, or age-related wear.

Other possible causes include bursitis, tendinitis, frozen shoulder, or arthritis. Symptoms may include sharp pain, weakness, limited range of motion, or a clicking sensation when lifting your arm.

Explanation

Shoulder pain when lifting your arm can occur for several reasons. One common cause is shoulder impingement, where the shoulder bones press on the tendons or bursa, causing pain. This can sometimes even cause neck pain as well. Another reason could be a rotator cuff tear, where some of the muscles and tendons that rotate your shoulder in place are damaged. The size of the tear can affect how much pain you feel and how much you can move your shoulder.

There are also specific conditions related to other health issues, like diabetes, that can cause shoulder pain. People with diabetes are prone to getting "Frozen Shoulder." This is a condition where the shoulder's range of motion becomes progressively more limited and painful.

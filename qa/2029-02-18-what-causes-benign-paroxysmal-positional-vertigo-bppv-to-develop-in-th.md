What causes Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) to develop in the first place?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Benign paroxysmal positional vertigo (BPPV) occurs when tiny calcium crystals (otoconia) inside the inner ear become dislodged and shift into the semicircular canals, where they don't belong. When you change head positions—such as lying down, rolling over, or looking up—these misplaced crystals disrupt normal fluid movement, sending false signals to your brain and triggering brief but intense episodes of dizziness or spinning.

Because dizziness can stem from many causes beyond BPPV—including migraines, blood pressure issues, or inner ear infections—identifying the right cause is essential for effective treatment. Normally, these crystals help you keep your balance. But when they move, they can cause dizziness, especially when you move your head in certain ways.

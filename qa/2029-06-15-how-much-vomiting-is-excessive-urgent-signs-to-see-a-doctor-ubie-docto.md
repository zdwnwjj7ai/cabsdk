How much vomiting is considered excessive?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Excessive vomiting is defined as frequent, uncontrollable vomiting that lasts for extended periods and can lead to dehydration, electrolyte imbalances, and other serious health complications. It is often a symptom of underlying conditions such as cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS), hyperemesis gravidarum during pregnancy, gastrointestinal disorders, or migraines. Because the causes vary widely, identifying the root issue is essential to getting the right treatment.

Explanation

Vomiting becomes excessive when it happens repeatedly and causes problems like dehydration or pain. This can be due to conditions such as cyclic vomiting syndrome, where vomiting occurs in episodes, or hyperemesis gravidarum, which is severe nausea and vomiting during pregnancy.

Internal Medicine

Yes, allergies can cause light sensitivity (photophobia). When allergens irritate your eyes, they trigger inflammation, swelling, and watering, which makes it harder for your eyes to tolerate bright light. Common culprits include pollen, dust, pet dander, and mold.

Because light sensitivity can also signal other conditions—like dry eye, migraines, or infections—it's worth pinpointing the real cause before assuming it's just allergies. Explanation

When you have allergies, your eyes can become red, itchy, and swollen due to inflammation, which is common in conditions like allergic conjunctivitis. This inflammation can make your eyes more sensitive to light, causing them to feel uncomfortable or even hurt when exposed to brightness.

Imagine how a small cut might make your skin feel extra tender; similarly, irritated eyes can react strongly to light. Some research even shows that the way light interacts with an allergic eye might make symptoms worse, especially when the allergy affects your eyes at night. If you notice that bright lights cause discomfort and you have allergies, use this free symptom checker to help identify whether inflammation in your eyes could be the cause.

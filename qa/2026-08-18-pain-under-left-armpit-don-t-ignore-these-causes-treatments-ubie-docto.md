What would cause pain under my left armpit and how can it be treated?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Pain under the left armpit is often caused by swollen lymph nodes, a benign fatty lump (lipoma) pressing on nearby nerves, or injury to the axillary nerve. Other common causes include muscle strain, skin infections, or irritation from shaving or deodorant. Because treatment depends entirely on the underlying cause, identifying the source of the pain is the essential first step.

Since left armpit pain can range from harmless irritation to something needing prompt medical attention, guessing isn't the safest approach. There are a few different reasons you might feel pain under your left armpit. One possibility is an axillary lipoma, which is a soft lump made of fat. Sometimes this lipoma can press on nearby tissues and even cause a condition called Complex Regional Pain Syndrome, where the pain spreads or feels stronger. Another cause could be an injury to the axillary nerve, which is a nerve that runs through the armpit. This type of injury can make the area hurt and may come from trauma or sometimes surgery. Swollen lymph nodes, or adenopathy, can also be the culprit; these nodes might swell when your body is fighting off an infection or inflammation, and this can cause discomfort. Treatment will depend on the cause. For a lipoma causing nerve pain, a doctor might recommend removing it or managing pain with medications. Nerve injuries often benefit from rest and physical therapy, sometimes along with pain relievers. If swollen lymph nodes are to blame, treating the underlying infection or inflammation will help.

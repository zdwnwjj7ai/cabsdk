# How an Expert Men's Health Trainer Helped Me Get My Whole Life Into Shape

As a former world missionary and teacher in a Tulsa suburb, I have built my life around trying to impact those around me. In 2017, at age 31, I created The American Dream Center, a nonprofit that helps immigrants navigate challenges and build happier, more productive lives. But because of the hours I was working and stress I was experiencing, my own wellbeing suffered.

Though I had run a full and half-marathon just a few years earlier, I was now very much out of shape. Luckily, I knew a guy. A college friend of mine, Sean Garner, N.S.C.A.-C.P.T., had spent years devoted to fitness, ran a multimillion-dollar gym and wellness facility in Miami, and was a Men’s Health Top Trainer contender. Over the past eight years, his Project DadBod system has helped hundreds of men reclaim their best bodies on limited time.

Okay, so maybe he wasn’t just any guy. But I could think of no better guy to help whip me back into shape. Thankfully, Sean was willing to take me on as a client, and created a plan for me to build stamina and strength. Although we didn’t live in the same city, he told me exactly what to do and when to do it, even giving me access to the Men’s Health All Out Studio app, which features videos of him properly executing each exercise. That feeling of accomplishment propelled me through each session.

Beginning to make changes

Ever heard of the Turkish get-up? Well, Sean told me to do it, and drew plenty of stares at the gym. But within only a couple weeks, I noticed major improvements in my mental and physical health. I was sleeping better; I’d fall asleep sooner and stay asleep through the night. My mood improved, and I was awash with energy, positivity, and mental clarity. So I decided I'd do whatever Sean told me to do moving forward.

Establishing a new routine

Looking for a goal to keep me committed and focused, I declared that I would complete a triathlon despite having limited cycling experience and almost no swimming skill to speak of. I immediately reached out to Sean for support. To build for my multi-sport challenge, my workouts became much more cardio-based, with a lot of biking, rowing, and squats. Sean continually checked on my progress and kept me focused through calls and texts. Those gym trips were now non-negotiable. My job remained demanding, but I found that only made my gym sessions more beneficial and worthwhile. There were more physical shifts—I gained muscle definition, especially in my shoulders from all that pulling on the rower—but my sleeping and mood also greatly improved.

I also lost weight, likely due to Sean’s equally valuable nutrition advice. Because of busy days and late-night hours at the office, I had often resorted to fast food. Sean stressed the importance of clean eating and introduced me to simple meal prep hacks. I’d cook chicken breasts and vegetables in bulk, divide individual portions into small containers, and bring them to the office.

A setback and reset

In March, with stay-at-home orders in effect and my pool and gym closing down, I fell back into old habits—lots of takeout, little time exercising. When the triathlon was cancelled, I was at a crossroads. But instead of quitting, and with Sean’s encouragement, I upped my goal.

Before, I’d hoped to finish the race. Now, since I have until next summer to train, I’m in it to compete. I dream of coming in first place for my age bracket.

With Sean’s guidance, I am now working out five to six days a week. Saturdays are my long training days where I run, bike, and swim. (I’m fortunate that my pool and gym re-opened a few weeks ago, though I’m sure Sean would find some other way to keep me grinding even if that hadn’t been the case.) The other days, I focus on lifting weights, usually for 45 minutes to an hour. I’m grateful to have something to work toward and to be following my own dream while leading my nonprofit to help others fulfill their dreams as well.

Is it Difficult to Lose Weight on a Vegan Diet?

Some studies suggest that a vegan diet can aid in weight loss. For example, a 2016 study found that people who followed a vegan diet decreased their consumption of saturated fats and lost more weight over six months than study participants who followed a vegetarian, semi-vegetarian, or omnivorous diet for the same amount of time.

Eating a vegetarian or vegan diet rich in vegetable fats may boost your metabolism. Improving your metabolic function can help you burn more calories while at rest, which can increase the effectiveness of your weight loss efforts.

If you eat a diet low in fat and low on glycemic index, you may be able to successfully lose weight on a vegan diet without counting calories.

Pay Attention to Portions and Food Groups

The United States Department of Agriculture (USDA) My Plate states that the average person needs the following portions of each food group daily:

Grains such as cooked cereal, pasta, or rice: 6 servings for women and nine servings for men.

Vegetables such as raw leafy greens or vegetable juice: 3 or more servings for women and four or more servings for men.

Fruits such as whole fruit or no-sugar-added fruit juice: 2 servings for women and three servings for men.

Dairy alternatives such as non-dairy milk: 2-3 servings for both men and women.

Meat alternatives, nuts, and beans such as soy burgers, peanut butter, and cooked dry beans: 5 ounces for women and 6 ounces for men.

Fats/oils such as butter and avocado: 5-6 servings for women and 6-7 servings for men.

Typically, one serving of each food group consists of one item (such as one orange or one piece of bread), one to two tablespoons, or one-half to two cups of a particular food.

Eat enough Protein

Eating about 5.5 ounces of protein per day is recommended, which amounts to about 0.41 grams per pound of your current body weight. There are about four calories in each gram of protein.

Prostate massage has been studied as a potential way to help with symptoms of benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH). Some small studies looked at repetitive prostate massage combined with drug therapy and found that it might help relieve lower urinary tract symptoms (such as difficulty starting or stopping urination) in a few men. There is also research examining at-home prostate massage devices. However, the evidence is limited and not definitive that prostate massage alone can treat or cure BPH. The effects seem to vary between individuals, and potential risks like irritation or infection might occur if not done properly. Because of these uncertainties, most doctors do not consider prostate massage a primary treatment for BPH. If you are interested in this approach or thinking of using an at-home device, it is important to discuss it with your healthcare provider. They can help determine whether it could be safe and potentially helpful in your overall management of BPH.

Ultra-Processed Foods

A 2024 study from Harvard made headlines when it found that people who ate the most UPFs had a 17 percent higher risk of coronary heart disease than people who ate the least. Processed meats and sugary drinks were the worst offenders. One telltale sign of an ultra-processed food is that the ingredients aren’t in recognizable form in the product—so you can’t see the corn in a corn chip, your turkey lunch meat doesn’t look anything like turkey, or there’s no sign of a strawberry in your strawberry Pop-Tart.

Added Sugar

Too much—especially from sodas, energy drinks, and sweets—is linked to inflammation, high triglycerides, insulin resistance, and a higher risk of heart attack, even in people who aren’t overweight. Aim for less than nine teaspoons (36 grams) of added sugar a day. (If you're having a hard time with that, try our painless 30-Day Eat Less Sugar Plan.)

Nothing Great

Beef Tallow

We’re seeing this pop up in kitchens to replace seed oils. But beef tallow is a saturated fat that raises LDL cholesterol, a major driver of plaque and heart disease. “While not uniquely harmful compared to other animal fats, there’s no evidence it supports heart health,” Dr. Fisher says.
